02 · Feeding the cycle

Malonyl-ACP, and the only ATP in the pathway

Every turn consumes one malonyl-ACP, made just upstream of the cycle (top right of the wheel). Acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACCase) uses biotin, ATP and bicarbonate to carboxylate acetyl-CoA to malonyl-CoA. Malonyl-CoA:ACP malonyltransferase (MCMT) then moves the malonyl group from CoA onto acyl carrier protein. From here on, everything happens on ACP: a small acidic protein whose 4′-phosphopantetheine arm holds each intermediate as a thioester and carries it between separate enzymes.

In the plastids of most plants ACCase is a heteromeric, bacterial-type complex of four subunits: biotin carboxyl carrier protein, biotin carboxylase, and the α and β subunits of carboxyltransferase. The β-carboxyltransferase gene, accD, is carried in the plastid genome. Grasses are the exception. Their plastid ACCase is a single large multidomain protein, like the cytosolic enzyme, and that difference is what grass-selective herbicides exploit.

ACCase catalyses the committed step and is where flux is controlled. It is activated in the light, as the stroma becomes more alkaline, Mg²⁺ rises, and thioredoxin reduces a disulfide in the carboxyltransferase. Accumulating acyl-ACP inhibits it, which matches supply to demand.

The bicarbonate is only on loan

The carbon that ACCase adds leaves again as CO₂ in the very next condensation, so none of it ends up in the fatty acid. What stays is the energy. A Claisen condensation between two ordinary thioesters is uphill; run in reverse, it is the thiolase step of β-oxidation. Decarboxylating malonyl makes the condensation strongly favourable, so the ATP spent by ACCase is what drives chain growth forward.

03 · The bill

What one fatty acid costs

Each turn costs one ATP (spent by ACCase) and two reductions: one NADPH at the keto reductase and one NADH at the enoyl reductase. The plastid enzymes split the reducing power this way; animal fatty acid synthase uses NADPH for both.

In a leaf, almost all of this comes from the light reactions or close to them. ATP and NADPH come from the thylakoid; the two reduced ferredoxins that the desaturase needs come straight from photosystem I. Much of the NADH comes from the plastid pyruvate dehydrogenase complex, the same reaction that supplies the acetyl-CoA. Add the light activation of ACCase, and it is no surprise that fatty acid synthesis in leaves runs far faster in the light than in the dark.

04 · Where the chain stops

Chain length is set by whoever reaches the acyl-ACP first

The cycle itself has no built-in stopping point. At every turn the saturated acyl-ACP is a branch point: a KAS can extend it, or another enzyme can take it away. In most plastids FatB competes with KAS II for 16:0-ACP, and almost everything that reaches 18:0-ACP is desaturated before FatA releases it as oleate. Change the competitor and you change the product, which is how a single California bay thioesterase turned rapeseed oil towards laurate.

Free fatty acids cross the plastid envelope and are reactivated as acyl-CoAs by long-chain acyl-CoA synthetases on the outer envelope. In the ER they become membrane phospholipids and, in seeds, triacylglycerol. Desaturation beyond 18:1 happens later, on lipids, not on ACP.

Q1Chloroplasts are given [1-¹⁴C]acetate, labelled on the carboxyl carbon, which they convert to [1-¹⁴C]acetyl-CoA. Which carbons of the palmitate they make are labelled?

All the odd-numbered carbons: C1, C3, C5 … C15. Acetyl C1 becomes the thioester carbon of malonyl-CoA, and therefore the new C1 of the chain in every turn. One turn later that carbon is C3, then C5, and so on. The primer's carbonyl carbon finishes at C15 and its methyl carbon at C16.

Switch the explorer to Colour carbons by: acetyl carbon and run to the end to see the alternation.

Q2A mixture of purified plastid enzymes (ACCase, MCMT and the four cycle enzymes) is given acetyl-CoA, ATP, NADPH, NADH, ACP and H¹⁴CO₃⁻. How much ¹⁴C ends up in the fatty acids? Why carboxylate at all?

None. The carbon ACCase adds is the free carboxyl of malonyl, which is released as CO₂ during condensation.

The carboxylation is there for thermodynamics. Decarboxylation of malonyl generates the carbanion that attacks the acyl thioester and makes the condensation strongly favourable; the condensation of two plain acetyl thioesters lies the other way, which is why thiolase cleaves in β-oxidation. The ATP spent by ACCase is the price of driving the chain forward.

In an intact leaf in the light, label from bicarbonate would of course reach fatty acids eventually, through photosynthesis, pyruvate and acetyl-CoA. That is a different route.

Q3List what one molecule of oleate costs a leaf chloroplast, and say where each input comes from in the light. Why does fatty acid synthesis slow sharply in the dark?

9 acetyl-CoA, 8 ATP, 8 NADPH, 8 NADH, 2 reduced ferredoxin and one O₂. ATP comes from the thylakoid ATP synthase; NADPH from linear electron flow through ferredoxin–NADP⁺ reductase; reduced ferredoxin for SAD directly from photosystem I; acetyl-CoA and much of the NADH from plastid pyruvate dehydrogenase.

In the dark the supply of ATP and reductant falls, and ACCase is switched down: the stroma acidifies, Mg²⁺ falls, and the thioredoxin-dependent reduction of the carboxyltransferase is lost.

Q4Cerulenin stops the chain at butyryl-ACP; triclosan stops it at crotonyl-ACP. Explain each. What would you find with both inhibitors present?

Cerulenin acylates the active-site cysteine of KAS I and KAS II. KAS III is insensitive, so the first turn completes and 4:0-ACP waits for a KAS I that cannot act. Triclosan blocks the enoyl reductase, so the first turn halts after three of its four steps with trans-Δ²-butenoyl (crotonyl)-ACP.

With both present, triclosan acts first: the chain never reaches butyryl-ACP, so cerulenin has nothing further to block. Crotonyl-ACP accumulates.

Q5Fluazifop controls grass weeds in a soybean field without harming the soybean. Explain using the structure of plastid ACCase.

Fops and dims bind the carboxyltransferase domain of the homomeric, multidomain ACCase. Grasses use that form in their plastids, so the herbicide stops malonyl-CoA production at the source and no fatty acid synthesis can start, not even the KAS III turn. Soybean, like other broadleaf plants, uses the heteromeric, bacterial-type ACCase in its plastids, which the herbicide does not inhibit. Compare the two herbicide options in the explorer.

Q6Coconut and California bay store oil rich in 12:0, yet their fatty acid synthase works like any other plant's. What determines chain length, and what single change made laurate-rich rapeseed?

Chain length is set by competition for each acyl-ACP between the next KAS and a termination enzyme. Plants that store medium-chain fatty acids express FatB thioesterases that hydrolyse 8- to 14-carbon acyl-ACPs before KAS I can extend them.

Expressing the California bay FatB in developing rapeseed seeds was enough to redirect much of the oil to laurate (Voelker et al. 1992). Run the laurate route and count the turns that are no longer used.

Q7Follow C3 through one turn. Give its oxidation state after each step, and say which steps are redox reactions.

After condensation C3 is a ketone carbon, +2. KAR reduces it to the alcohol, 0. HAD takes it to −1 in the C2=C3 alkene, but C2 goes from −2 to −1 at the same time, so the dehydration is not a redox reaction overall. ENR takes both C2 and C3 to −2.

So KAR and ENR are the two reductions, each adding two electrons. The four electrons per turn come from NADPH and NADH.

Q8Plants use a type II synthase (separate enzymes, intermediates on a free ACP); animals use a type I synthase (one large multifunctional protein). Suggest an advantage of type II for a plastid.

Every acyl-ACP is accessible to other enzymes. Thioesterases with different chain-length preferences, the soluble desaturase, the plastid acyltransferases, and the octanoyltransferase that starts lipoic acid synthesis can all draw intermediates off the same pool. Separate KAS isoforms with different specificities can be regulated and engineered independently. In a type I synthase the chain stays on the complex until it is released, so there is far less room for branching.

Q9The ER fatty acid elongase, which makes very-long-chain fatty acids for cuticular wax, uses the same four reactions. What is different about its carrier and its source of two-carbon units?

The elongase works on acyl-CoAs, not acyl-ACPs, and takes its two-carbon units directly from malonyl-CoA made by the cytosolic, homomeric ACCase; no ACP is involved. Its four enzymes (3-ketoacyl-CoA synthase, 3-ketoacyl-CoA reductase, 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydratase and enoyl-CoA reductase) are membrane-bound in the ER, and the ketoacyl-CoA synthase sets the final chain length.
